Project Management

Identification

All project stakeholders were systematically identified and formally classified at initiation to ensure comprehensive coverage across every delivery dimension.

Internal stakeholders spanned project leadership, software development, drone engineering, data analytics, finance, legal, sales and marketing, customer support, and the innovation function.

External stakeholders comprised farmers as primary end users, investors, Irish and EU regulatory authorities, hardware and software suppliers, agricultural consultants, government and policy bodies, insurance providers, and industry associations.


Analysis

Power / Interest Grid

Each stakeholder was assessed across two dimensions organisational power and project interest to determine the appropriate engagement strategy and resource allocation.

The objective of this campaign was to address that gap. Over a 12-month period, the strategy was designed to grow active membership, establish a viable online revenue stream, and deepen community engagement without compromising the authenticity that underpins the Co-op's brand equity.


Stakeholder Register

A formal Stakeholder Register was maintained throughout the project lifecycle, providing a single source of truth for roles, classifications, and designated contacts ensuring full accountability and traceability at every stage.


Expectation Management

An Expectation Management Matrix was established to align all parties on project priorities across five dimensions.

Quality and customer satisfaction were designated non-negotiable priorities. Farmers were required to derive measurable, tangible value from the system no compromise on this standard was accepted. Technology alignment between engineering and innovation functions was equally critical, given its direct impact on solution accuracy and market competitiveness. Scope was governed by fixed core requirements, with controlled variation permitted to accommodate individual farm configurations. Schedule, while actively monitored, was treated as the lowest priority dimension, with formal escalation protocols governing any deviation.


Communication Plan

A structured communication plan defined channels, frequencies, and accountability for every stakeholder group, ensuring consistent information flow and timely escalation throughout delivery.


Governance operated through weekly standups, formal phase gate reviews, dedicated change control sessions, and structured stakeholder feedback meetings maintaining alignment and decision integrity at each stage of the project lifecycle.


Conclusion

Through the disciplined application of a formal stakeholder register, a structured expectation matrix, and a comprehensive communication plan, all parties were engaged in proportion to their influence and interest throughout the project. This governance approach directly underpinned consistent decision-making, proactive risk escalation, and a shared, well-defined understanding of success from initiation through to final delivery.
